How do you choose a dropshipping supplier from Brazil?

Choosing a dropshipping supplier in Brazil isn't always easy, but we have some tips to help you find the perfect one for your store.

First, if it's a supplier located in Brazil, make sure they're legit.

In Brazil, every legal business must have a CNPJ (Cadastro Nacional da Pessoa Jurídica).

You can think of it as a business ID number.

Before you sign up, make sure your supplier has a valid CNPJ. You can easily check this on the Receita Federal website.

Another thing you can consider is your supplier's location.

Brazil is huge, so suppliers based in São Paulo can probably offer much faster logistics, while those in remote areas have longer delivery times.

Another thing to keep in mind is the taxes.

Brazil's tax system is notoriously complex.

When dealing with dropshipping suppliers, taxes like ICMS (state tax) and IPI (industrialized product tax) apply.

A good supplier should:

Be transparent about pricing, including any taxes.
Provide Nota Fiscal (tax invoice), which is legally required for all sales in Brazil.

Lastly, customer support is always good to check. WhatsApp is super popular in Brazil, so check if they offer WhatsApp support.

Apply these tips to your list of suppliers, and see who stands out!

Does dropshipping work in Brazil?

Short answer: Yes, dropshipping works in Brazil.

But like anything in business, success depends on how you approach it

Brazil has a huge population (over 200 million people), and a growing number of them are shopping online:

Platforms like Mercado Livre, Amazon Brasil, and Shopee dominate, so you don't need to set up your own website immediately.

You can start by listing your products on popular marketplaces and gradually build your brand.

Where it gets tricky is usually in the shipping.

The country's size and infrastructure mean delivery times vary widely depending on where your customer lives.

Shipping from São Paulo to Rio de Janeiro?

Pretty quick.

Shipping to the Amazon region?

That will take (a lot) longer.

As a last tip, know that Brazilians love their boleto bancário and installments.

Yes, credit cards are also used, but offering flexible payment options can boost your sales significantly.
