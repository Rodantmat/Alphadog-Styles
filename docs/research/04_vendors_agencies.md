## (b) Consolidated findings by theme (with conflicts and conflict-of-interest flags)

**1. Hook window and early hold.** Nearly every source says the first 1.5-3 s decide scroll vs stay (rows 3, 17, 28, 46). Hard evidence is thin: the 1.5 s and "80-90% fail" claims are unsourced (rows 2, 3). The best-supported number is the Meta-attributed 65% of viewers who pass 3 s watch 10+ s (row 28, secondary). Hook-rate benchmarks conflict by platform and vendor: >30% strong / <20% kill (row 13); 40%+ target / <30% kill (row 60); 25-30% baseline Meta, 30-35% TikTok, 40%+ elite (row 17); game ads target 65% (row 21). All are ad-account norms, not organic benchmarks, and none are for 10 s slideshows. Conflict: unmeasurable across sources; use own account as baseline.

**2. Hook TYPE ranking is source-dependent and sometimes contradictory.**
- OpusClip (n=34,635, correlational): product/outcome showcase ranks first by views (~2x lowest) (rows 1-2). Source is talking-head clip data; "product showcase" winning is relevant to apparel but is a vendor-defined taxonomy.
- Quso (121K hooks, medians): social-proof openers highest; authority and direct-command lowest (row 38). Opus lists "Imperative Command" in its top 5 (row 2). Direct conflict on command-style hooks; neither controls for creator size.
- Motion (550k Meta ads, BFCM only): newness, sale, price anchor, urgency win on hit rate; bold claim worst (row 6). That is paid ads in a sale window, so it favours offer hooks that organic reels may not reward.
- Klap citing a 46,605 TikTok hook study: curiosity gap and urgency beat direct-address/instructional in most categories (row 39).
- Verdict: no tested winner across sources. Convergent claims (all C-grade): specific/concrete beats vague; open-loop curiosity works; filler openers ("Hi guys", "Have you ever wondered") are weak (row 5).

**3. Specificity.** Supported by vidIQ's large title study: specific story promises (>2-4x) beat generic formulas, plain how-to titles ~1.07x, question-ending titles 0.82x (row 31). Air.io: numbers help only when they carry a specific promise (row 35). ClipSpeedAI: direct, specific first-2-s statement holds 67% more viewers to 30 s (row 29, vendor CEO, unverifiable). Convergent but all observational and YouTube/long-ish, not Reels.

**4. Captions and on-screen text: presence is well supported, size of lift is not.**
- Best-sourced: Verizon/Publicis 2019 survey (n=5,616): 80% more likely to watch to end with captions; +8% recall, +13% brand linkage (row 8); Meta: captions +12% view time on video ads (rows 7, 28); Discovery/3Play: +7.32% YouTube views, +13.48% in first 14 days (row 8). These are old (2016-2019) and mostly about speech video, not silent product slideshows.
- Caption-style percentages (+15-40%, word-by-word best) in rows 9, 10, 26, 27, 57 have no method and are generated by caption-tool sellers (Opus, MakeAIClips, Vizard, Eliro). Treat as zero evidence. Several contradict themselves (12% vs 39% in row 27).
- Prevalence: 80.2% of viral-tier clips have captions, but only ~4.5% of all clips use dedicated text overlays (rows 1, 4). Implication: caption presence is table stakes; a standalone overlay line is rare, but no lift is quantified.
- First-frame text: ~63-70% of top TikTok ads have text in frame 1 (rows 13, 17); descriptive of selected winners, no baseline for non-winners.
- Placement: a platform-UI-safe-zone argument (avoid bottom third) is repeated (rows 10, 17); only row 10's "-10-15%" is numeric and unsourced. Merch caution: text must not cover product (owner rule).
- Emoji: conflicting. vidIQ (128M videos): emoji in Shorts titles +49% mean views, +13% median, +24% comments (row 32), but this is correlational and sits in YouTube titles. Quso: TikTok captions with emoji had LOWER median views (228 vs 357) (row 38). Eliro/others: +5-12% (unsourced). Conflict unresolved; keep emoji count to 0-2 and test.

**5. Length and completion.** Vidyard/Wistia (B2B hosted video, large n): under 1 min = 52-66% watched; peak engagement in first quarter (rows 16, 40, 48, 54). POD sources recommend 7-15 s (rows 49-51, no data). A 10 s reel is inside every recommended range.

**6. CTA wording.** Almost no hard data.
- Only numeric CTA test claims: "Shop the Look" +30% CTR vs "See What's New" (row 23, unsourced anecdote); CTA cards +45% recall, +19% likeability vs plain text overlay (row 22, cited to TikTok research, n.s.).
- Wistia: interactive CTA at end of video works best; lead-gen forms highest click rate (row 16, B2B, not retail).
- Guidance: verb-first, <=5 words, benefit-led, final 2 s (row 22); soft "Link in bio" works for $75 apparel at ~25-30 s (row 60); price/offer in first 3 s for promo hooks (row 46).
- Attention vs conversion can diverge: the stronger 6-s-view hook converted worse (1.2% vs 1.8% ATC) in one illustrative template (row 20). Do not pick hooks on view rate alone.

**7. Social proof / price / offer wording.** Motion: price anchor 10.89% hit rate with 2.0 spend-use ratio, urgency 9.73%, FOMO 9.20% (row 6, BFCM bias). Quso: social-proof openers have the highest median views (row 38). Apparel playbook (TikTok): "4 Million Pairs sold + Shop Now" style proof (row 46). For a small new brand with no sales figures, proof must be real (do not fabricate counts).

**8. UGC / human framing.** Many vendors say human/creator beats polished (rows 12, 14, 17, 23, 44) with numbers that are mostly unsourced (4x CTR, 33% better direct address). Motion says image+text overlay and text-only ads produced more winners than high-production ads (row 6). Slideshow of product photos is not UGC; the closest transferable element is caption voice (first-person, comment-reply, "POV").

**9. Pinterest.** Tailwind (1.2M pins): titles with keywords present in ~80% of viral pins, titles are visible only first ~35-45 chars, descriptions short (~220-232 chars) and single-topic; video only 8% of viral pins, vertical 2:3+ dominates (row 53). It is descriptive (what viral pins look like), not a lift test. Implication: put the keyword (dog breed + garment) at the front of the pin title.

**10. YouTube Shorts.** vidIQ: median Shorts views do not rise with experience, volume increases the chance of one outlier (row 34); title frames "Turning X into Y", "Caught on camera", "Nobody knows" far above average; question-ending titles slightly below average (rows 31, 35). Titles studied are mostly long-form+Shorts mixed; frame lists apply best to Shorts titles but are not Shorts-only.

**11. Testing hygiene (agencies).** Common Thread (170+ brands): ~3.5% of ads are outliers, ~79% die before $1,000, fashion/lifestyle outlier rate 2-3% (row 42). Meaning: expect most hook variants to fail, plan volume (3-5 variants per concept) not perfection. Min sample ~1,000-2,000 impressions per variant for hook-rate reads (rows 13, 20, 60).

**Conflict-of-interest flags.** Strongest: OpusClip (rows 1-5, 9, 18, 57; sells hook/caption AI and ranks itself first), Kapwing/Vizard/Eliro/MakeAIClips (caption tools), Winning Hunter/Foreplay/Motion/Segwise (ad-spy/analytics, sell hook libraries and testing), Splice/CapCut (editors), Superside/agencies (sell creative). Larger-dataset vendors (vidIQ, Tailwind, Vidyard, Wistia, Motion, Common Thread) disclose sample sizes but are observational. No source here ran a randomized wording test on organic short-form video.

## (c) Concrete wording implications for a dog apparel brand (10 s product-photo slideshow reels)

Evidence caveat: none of this was tested on dog-apparel slideshows. Treat every template as a hypothesis to A/B in the brand's own account.

**Principles with the best support**
1. Make frame 1 carry specific text (not a logo or "new drop"). ~63-70% of top TikTok ads had first-frame text (rows 13, 17); sound-off viewing is large (Verizon/Publicis: 69% in public) (row 8). Text must not cover the garment.
2. Be specific and concrete over generic: vidIQ title frames (row 31), Opus "front-load specificity" (row 3), ClipSpeedAI (row 29). Name the breed ("French Bulldog mom") rather than "dog lovers".
3. Open a loop or promise within 10 s: curiosity-gap hooks beat instructional ones (rows 38-39, 5). Slideshow loop: first frame states a claim, last frame resolves it.
4. Do not open with filler ("Hi guys", "Have you ever wondered", "Okay so") (row 5, C-grade).
5. Avoid question-ending titles for Shorts (0.82x vs average) unless the viewer already holds the question (rows 31, 35). Plain descriptive titles ("Dog shirt") are ~1.0x, i.e. no help.
6. Pinterest: breed + garment keyword in the first ~35 chars of the pin title; short single-topic description (~220 chars) (row 53).
7. Do not judge hooks only by view rate; check saves/clicks/ATC too (row 20).
8. Real proof only: social-proof openers had highest median views (row 38), but fabricated counts are unacceptable; use real review counts or "ships in X days" facts.

**Things the evidence does not support (avoid claiming)**
- Any precise "+X% from word-by-word captions" figure (rows 9, 10, 26, 27, 57).
- That emoji in captions help: vidIQ says Shorts titles with emoji did better, Quso says TikTok captions with emoji did worse (rows 32, 38).
- That questions in titles help: vidIQ 0.82x (row 31).
- That sale/urgency hooks transfer to organic reels: the strongest data (Motion) is paid Meta ads in Black Friday-Christmas (row 6).

**Suggested test design (from rows 13, 20, 42, 60):** same slideshow, same music, change only frame-1 text; 3-5 hook variants per design; at least ~1,000-2,000 impressions per variant before cutting; judge on 3-s hold, completion, saves/sends, and clicks, not views alone; expect 2-3% of concepts to become outliers (row 42).
