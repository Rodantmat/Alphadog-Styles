## (b) Consolidated findings

### 1. Audience psychology and identity
- **The dog is family, and for young owners a child.** Rover Feb 2026 (n=1,000): 88% "part of the family"; 66% of Gen Z see pets as kids while older owners say "best friend". MetLife 2026 (n=1,006): 65% use "mom/dad"; Gen Z 76% baby-talk. Vilnius study: 66.7% call pets children.
- **The label is gendered and not universal.** YouGov US Jan 2025 (n=2,223, best-quality source here): dog owners' self-labels are companion 49%, best friend 46%, owner 46%, parent 39%; women 52% vs men 26% say "parent". MetLife: mom/dad 74% women, 49% men. YouGov UK: 43% women / 29% men. Implication: "dog mom" works strongly for women; men respond better to "dog dad" framed with humour or to "best friend"/"companion"/neutral breed-pride wording. A men's line should not rely on "parent" language alone.
- **The label can be rejected.** Dogster and the academic work show some people avoid "owner" (control) and "parent" (child-substitute). Safe neutral hooks: "dog person", the breed name, "my best friend", "his human/her human".
- **Identity is expressed publicly and often.** Rover: 40% own dog-mom apparel, another 17% want it (55% of Gen Z dog moms own dog apparel); 62% take dozens of photos; 29% post more dog photos than family; ~50% talk enthusiastically about their dog to new people; 56% celebrate birthdays; MetLife 33% throw gotcha-day parties (Chihuahua owners 53%). Wearing apparel is a "talking about my dog" behaviour that people already do.
- **Breed choice is a self-portrait.** Rover July 2024: 61% chose their dog for assumed breed traits; 71% say breed choice reflects personality; 52% share traits with their dog (anxiety most common). Breed-trait humour therefore doubles as self-description.
- **Gifting is large but mostly for the dog, not for the human.** Chewy 2025: 78% buy the pet a gift; PetSafe 2020: 96%; >40% spent more on pets than people; Circana: ~4.4 gifts per pet per year, 5.46 for under-33s. None of the reports sized gifts FOR dog owners (apparel). Cross-over wording that targets the occasion (birthday, gotcha day, Mother's/Father's Day, Christmas "from the dog") is supported by behaviour, but no pet-industry source here sizes the human-apparel gift market; treat as untested.
- **Market backdrop.** APPA: 71M dog households (53%), $158B 2025. APPA's CEO frames 2026 as "more intentional spending" and the report lists no apparel category: expect price sensitivity; Printify (weak source) claims breed-specific matching apparel +41% 2022-25.
- **Emotional uses of dogs.** 3 in 4 use dog content to lift mood (Rover); Pet content is "inspiration or escape" (SmallBizTrends); communities value warm, upbeat tone (HeyAlma). Negative or preachy copy is off-brand.

### 2. Tone
- **Winning brand voice = dog-first with a human wink, never prim.** BarkBox: "Be the person your dog thinks you are.", "Free Shipping Forever. You pay zero human dollars.", "Wanna sniff butts?" signup; "We've never fooled ourselves into thinking we should be prim and proper." Voice is applied even in signup/FAQ copy (mediacat).
- **Self-deprecating owner voice** ("Best friend, worst alarm clock.") and **teasing-the-humans** voice (HeyAlma) both outperform corporate copy in the sources, but no source gives controlled A/B numbers. Treat as pattern evidence only.
- **Warm positive tone** is the default; topical or political quips exist but are polarising.

### 3. Humour mechanics that recur
1. **Dog-POV first person** ("I sat. Where's my modeling fee?", "I have decided that the rug is my enemy.", "What's the cutest thing about my face?").
2. **Dog gets human job/status** ("CEO: Chief Ears Officer").
3. **Puns on dog vocabulary** (paws/fur/ruff/pup/mutt/retrieve; "Sit. Stay. Slay."). Used at scale in caption lists but they read as generic; the stronger examples twist a breed word ("Don't Stop, Retrievin'", "We Shiz Tzu Not!").
4. **Exaggerated stakes/hyperbole** ("loved at maximum settings", 5-minute absence = major loss).
5. **Coined dog terms** (zoomies o'clock, snoot booped, splooting, doge).
6. **Relatable-micro-moment (specificity)**: "hears the cheese drawer from three rooms away", "POV: When I hear the snack bag", rolling in smelly things.
7. **Breed-as-person / breed-as-partner formats**: "If dog breeds were people" (40M+ views, series numbered, "so accurate", "if you've ever met a Bernese..."), "I don't have a golden retriever husband, I have a [breed] husband" (Yorkie: small, barks at everything because terrified; GSD: loyal, tough love).
8. **Generational comparison skits** (Boomer/Gen X/Millennial/Gen Z dog care) with self-deprecating comment replies.
9. **Mock-indignant / absurd brand bits** (BarkBox "Squirrel Invasion", mock "liars" post).
10. **Talking to the dog as a person with a backstory** (Shih Tzu pep talk: "you're NOT from the Bronx").

### 4. Breed-specific hooks (which breeds, what people say)
- **Popularity (registrations):** French Bulldog 1 (4 years running), Lab, Golden, GSD, Dachshund, Poodle, Beagle, Rottweiler, GSP, Bulldog, Cane Corso (rising 14 to 11), Cavalier, Yorkie, Aussie, Doberman, Corgi, Mini Schnauzer, Boxer, Pomeranian (22 to 19), Bernese (AKC 2025).
- **Search interest differs from registrations:** worldwide Google volume (keyword planner, PR source) puts Cane Corso top (4.09M/mo), then Golden, Rottweiler, Dobermann, Border Collie, GSP, Pomeranian, Shih Tzu; adoption-site searches put Chihuahua, Lab, Dachshund, GSD, Pit Bull on top. Rover: mixed breeds are the most popular "breed", Mini Poodle fastest trending 2024. Mixed/rescue owners are a large unaddressed identity group.
- **Stereotype hooks in circulation:** Golden = wholesome goofball ("golden retriever energy/husband" template); Husky = dramatic, talks back; Chihuahua = small dog big attitude, dramatic; Frenchie = calm/chic/trendsetter, meme icon; Pug = perpetually surprised; Bulldog = grumpy old man, couch potato; Corgi = short legs, splooting; Dachshund = "wiener", squeezes into tiny spaces; Shiba = skeptical, "doge"; Boxer = goofy, confused-optimistic; Yorkie = tiny and terrified/furious; GSD = loyal, tough love, serious; Bernese = gentle giant (used in "if you've ever met a Bernese" line); Shih Tzu = pigtails, attitude. Rover: loyalty most wanted trait (Pit Bull, GSD, Chihuahua).
- **Breed-targeted hashtags and niche SEO** are an explicit tactic (BARK: "cavapoo", "blue heeler").

### 5. Engagement mechanics for CTAs and comments (pet sources)
- **Sends/saves over likes**; "tag 3 friends"/"comment YES" bait is described as penalised (Dogfluence, citing Instagram head's Jan 2025 remarks). Give a specific reason to send: "send this to someone whose [breed] also..." instead of "tag a friend".
- **Questions** about the dog drive replies; a question in a creator reply gets a fan answer back 37% vs 8% (Dogfluence, n=12,000 reply pairs, correlational). Specific questions ("Does your dog do this too?", "Rate this outfit 1-10", "What's your pet's funniest habit?") recur but evidence is vendor-level.
- **Reply to nearly every comment**, use the person's name, leave a first comment of your own: +50%/+44%/+19% on engagement rate (correlational).
- **Giveaway format** (follow, like, tag friends) produces reach but is paid-for engagement (PrideBites 73,817 impressions, 4,284 engagements).
- **First 1-3 seconds:** open mid-moment, on the dog's face/eyes; on-screen text frames the situation; no "hey guys". Reels 5-9 s or 7-15 s advised (unsourced).
- **Pet content lifts brand comments** (NewsWhip 2016: +89% comments avg across 15 brands), and comments, not likes, are the dog-content signal; carousels draw more comments (48 vs 22 for Reels, Dogfluence) while Reels drive discovery.
- **UGC wins:** BARK's top 9 of 10 Instagram posts were UGC (RivalIQ). For a POD brand: re-share customer photos of dogs wearing the tee; ask for the breed in the caption.
- **Opening 125 characters** of an IG caption are the visible headline (Dogfluence).

### 6. Caveats and gaps
- No source here tested apparel-specific hooks. Most caption "evidence" is vendor guidance (C/D grade). Highest-quality numbers: YouGov US 2025, APPA, MetLife/Rover (large-ish n, but commissioned).
- Brand-voice sources describe style; none report A/B engagement for puns vs. non-puns.
- Breed search ranking evidence conflicts across sources (registrations vs adoption vs keyword volume); do not treat any one as the "top breeds" truth. Use own analytics.
- Search volume for specific phrases ("dog mom shirt") was not found on a fetchable page.

## (c) Concrete wording implications for a dog-apparel brand (45 phrases)

Rule of thumb from the evidence: (1) name the breed, (2) one specific relatable micro-moment or trait, (3) write as the dog or as the proud/self-deprecating human, (4) let a human choose whether to send, not "tag 3".

### On-screen hooks (first 1-2 seconds)
1. "POV: you own a Golden and nobody warned you about the pure chaos." - POV + breed + relatable (miracamp/dogfluence pattern).
2. "If dog breeds were people: Bernese edition" - numbered series format that drove 40M+ views; make it a recurring series per breed.
3. "I don't have a golden retriever husband, I have a Yorkie husband." - viral breed-as-partner template; fits the breeds on the hoodie.
4. "Husky owners, you know why this hoodie exists." - breed callout (relatable, narrows audience, matches breed-identity data: 71% say breed reflects personality).
5. "Things only Frenchie owners understand:" - "only X owners" format, breed-first.
6. "Your Dachshund hears the treat bag from three rooms away. We made a hoodie for that." - specificity (cheese drawer example) plus product tie-in.
7. "Why does my Corgi do this?" over a splooting clip, with tee in frame - question hook plus known breed meme.
8. "Rate this hoodie 1-10 (he gave it a 10)." - "Rate this outfit 1-10" prompt, dog-voice twist.
9. "He said NO to the cap. Then he saw the treat." - mid-moment story, no throat-clear.
10. "Pug face, zero thoughts. Pug hoodie, maximum thoughts." - pug "perpetually surprised" trope.
11. "Small dog, big attitude: the Chihuahua tee" - established trope, caption-ready.
12. "Gotcha day deserves a hoodie." - gotcha-day parties (33% of owners; Chihuahua owners 53%).
13. "Dog mom starter pack:" (list of objects incl. lint roller + your tee) - lint-roller and poop-bag jokes were BarkBox's dog-mom rap beats.
14. "Dog dads aren't 'parents', they're his best friend." - respects men's lower "parent" use (26% vs 52%).
15. "Wait for it... he never misses the zoomies." - zoomies as a universal hook; loop ending.

### Dog-voice captions (text as the dog)
16. "I sat. Where's my modeling fee?" (tee on dog or owner)
17. "My human bought the matching hoodie. I have decided I'm the better-dressed one."
18. "I have decided the vacuum is my enemy. Anyway, she's wearing my face on her chest."
19. "Dear human: the cap is cute. The treat is better."
20. "I don't make the rules. I just sit on them." (sit-on-your-lap theme)
21. "What's the cutest thing about my face? (It's on your shirt.)" - adapted from Doug the Pug's line; line art makes the literal joke work.
22. "Best friend, worst alarm clock." - proud-parent self-deprecation, short.
23. "Loved at maximum settings." - hyperbole one-liner for a heart-icon design.

### Owner-identity captions (short, 2-8 words)
24. "Dog mom, no apologies." (women's identity, 74% mom/dad use among women)
25. "Proud Frenchie dad." (breed + gender)
26. "Ask me about my Golden." - mirrors "50% talk enthusiastically about their dog" behaviour.
27. "Fur baby on board." (fur baby/fur kid used by 45% of dog moms)
28. "Just a person who really loves their Beagle."
29. "Wears fur like it's a fashion statement." (fur-as-fashion pattern)
30. "Dog person. Dachshund person, specifically."
31. "Rescued. Still not over it." for mixed/rescue owners; do not forget mixed breeds as a large identity.

### CTAs and comment prompts (share-reason, not bait)
40. "Send this to the person whose Husky also argues with the doorbell." - give-a-reason-to-send (Dogfluence example format).
41. "Save this for your next gifting panic (gotcha day, Mother's Day, Christmas)." - save reason; gift occasions backed by birthday/holiday data.
42. "What breed should we draw next?" - low-effort, specific question; a question reply gets answered 37% vs 8%.
43. "Which one is your dog? 1, 2, or 3" over three breed designs - choice prompt, easier to answer than open question.
44. "Does your dog do this too?" - repeated vendor pattern; use on trait-led clips.
45. "Show us yours: tag a pic of your dog in the tee and we'll feature it." - UGC prompt; BARK's top posts were UGC.
46. Reply template to comments: "[Name]! [Dog's name] approves." - using names/dog's name (+19% engagement correlation).

### Titles / on-pack and bio copy
47. Bio: "Breed line art for people who talk about their dog too much." - echoes the 50%-talk-enthusiastically behaviour.
48. Product title pattern: "[Breed] Mom Hoodie - [one-line trait]" e.g. "Corgi Mom Hoodie - Short Legs, Big Plans" (breed first for search; breed hashtags/niche SEO are an explicit tactic).
49. Signup/footer in voice: "Wanna sniff out new designs?" - BarkBox's "Wanna sniff butts?" shows voice in microcopy increases memorability; keep it clean but on-voice.

### Reasoning summary
- Use breed names early; breed is self-expression (71%) and the strongest viral templates are breed-as-person formats.
- Mix three registers: dog-POV, proud-human, and trait-roast; avoid corporate.
- Specific beats generic ("cheese drawer", "snack bag") and short beats long (most caption examples are 2-5 words).
- Gate language by audience: "mom" for women, "dog dad"/"best friend"/breed pride for men, neutral "dog person" for everyone else.
- CTAs: give a reason to send or save; reserve "tag 3" and "comment YES" (reported as penalised).
- Series and numbered parts plus replying to comments held audiences (spoof series, reply-engagement data).
