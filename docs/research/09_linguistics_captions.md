## (b) Consolidated findings

**Caption length.** Three big datasets agree short is better for video: Socialinsider 2026 (9.7M TikToks) ER falls monotonically with length: under 30 words 4.9, 30-60 4.5, 60-90 4.3, 90+ 3.9 (about 20% drop from short to 90+). IG Reels are flat-ish: 0.20 under 30 words, 0.25 at 30-60 and 60-90, 0.20 at 90+, so moderate (30-90 words) is slightly ahead for Reels, but the under-30 and 90+ ends are equal. HubSpot/37M posts: 1-50 characters best in North America/UK (21-50), 1-20 globally; no caption is the worst. Quintly: 1-50 chars best. Practical sweet spot: a one-liner of 20-150 characters, with the hook inside the first ~125 characters (IG cut-off) and 80-100 chars on TikTok. Small Hootsuite tests (n=10 reels) favour short but are anecdotal. Long captions earn more comments on photo posts, not on video.

**On-screen text.** The best evidence for text on video: arXiv TikTok study found text on 67% of viral vs 30% of non-viral videos (correlational; creator size is the dominant confound). Sound-off: 69% sound-off in public, 25% in private, 92% of US mobile viewers sound-off at least sometimes, Meta 85% of Facebook video muted. Captions: +12% view time (Meta, old), ad recall +8%, brand linkage +13%. Caveat: Verizon/Publicis found captions + logo both prominent cut ad memory 12%, so do not stack text and logo. One JMIR FB trial found subtitles on a short portrait clip did not help; text should be a hook, not transcript.

**Emoji.** Mixed and weak. Older datasets (quintly 2016, 6.2M posts) +17% interaction with emoji; HubSpot 2022 (37M posts) emoji vs none <1% difference, 1-10 emoji small boost, 11+ no benefit; Agorapulse test not significant; quso 2026 (508K posts) shows lower median views for emoji captions (TikTok 228 vs 357), likely confounded. Conclusion: emoji is neutral; use 1-3 purposeful ones (paw, dog), never a wall.

**Hashtags.** Socialinsider 75M posts: 3-4 hashtags best (3.41%), more tags lowered ER for most account sizes; 5-6 for 5K-50K. Hootsuite test: 3-5 beat 30 on reach by ~15%. arXiv TikTok: hashtag stuffing has little effect, hashtag features are weak predictors. HubSpot found >11 and 4 highest, so noisy. Consensus: 3-5 targeted hashtags (1 breed, 1 niche, 1 category, optionally 1 broad). Pinterest: viral pins only 19% used hashtags; keyword-rich title >=25 chars, description ~220-230 chars, <=5 keywords.

**Punctuation / capitalization / readability.** No clean large dataset on punctuation was found. All-caps: slower reading, recall hurt for 7-8 word headlines, no harm for 3-4 words; capitalise 1-2 key words only (Willemsen & Neijens 2024, via vendor summary). Accessibility: WCAG 4.5:1 contrast normal text, 3:1 for large text (>=24px); text over photos is measured against the actual background; dyslexia guidance: avoid italics, 1.5 line spacing, +0.03em tracking, short sentences, 50-75 chars per line.

**Subtitle pacing.** Netflix 17 cps adult (13 cps children), BBC 160-180 wpm (~15-17 cps), min ~0.3 s/word, max 2 lines. For a 10 s reel at 17 cps the whole video holds at most ~170 characters (~30 words) of on-screen reading material total. A 3-4 beat structure of 3-6 words per card is within limits.

**Numerals.** BuzzSumo (100M headlines, 2017) best list numbers: 10, 5, 15, 7, 20 (Facebook engagement). For a reel this suggests numerals in hooks ("5 dog breeds...", "10 reasons") but evidence is for article headlines, not video. Adobe/TikTok search: tutorials 61%, reviews 45%.

**Pronouns / emotion.** Stanford (272K comments + experiments): "you" backfires in conflict/negative messages, neutral in friendly acknowledgement; arXiv TikTok: second-person POV in 70% of viral vs 60% of non-viral videos. Berger & Milkman (6,956 articles): high-arousal emotions (awe, amusement, anger) and practical utility boost sharing, low-arousal sadness lowers it (-.17). Positive tone slightly stronger than negative (.13). Implication: warm, "you/your dog" friendly language is fine; avoid accusatory "you".

**Bilingual / Spanish / Portuguese.** TikTok US Hispanic survey: >half of bilinguals prefer both languages, 2/3 feel closer to brands using multiple languages, Spanish-dominant viewers 57% more likely to watch more ads after Spanish voiceover, 3 in 4 find Spanish music appealing; Nielsen-type data: bilingual ads appeal to 7 in 10 Latinos on Spanish TV, more than half on English TV; non-Hispanic 18-49 are 41-44% appealing vs 15-17% turnoff, but 50+ lean 39% turnoff, so keep bilingual light. Brazil: 57% more likely to buy when product presented in own language, 60% would switch brand for native support (RWS 2020, old); informal register is preferred by consumers in Portugal (63% "tu"; 84% young) which suggests Brazilian brand voice should be informal "voce" (direction only, not Brazil data). Brazil social use: Instagram 49%, WhatsApp 46% weekly for news. I found no rigorous pt-BR caption-length study; pt-BR blog advice (first 150 chars, 3-5 hashtags, 1-3 lines of on-screen text) is unsourced.

**Not found / gaps.** No good dataset for punctuation (exclamation, question marks) in video captions, and no good dataset on second-person pronouns in product reels. Netflix style-guide pages were blocked; figures come via secondary summaries.

## (c) Concrete wording implications for a dog apparel brand

1. **Caption = one short line, under ~125 characters, hook first** (e.g. "Your dog, but make it a hoodie"). TikTok under 30 words has the highest ER; the IG fold hides everything after ~125 characters. Put the breed word in the first line for search (Adobe: 49% of US consumers search on TikTok).
2. **On-screen text: 3-5 beat cards, 3-6 words each, whole video <=~30 words** (17 cps rule). Beat 1 is the hook text in first second; last beat is CTA. Keep text high-contrast (4.5:1+, put a dark box or stroke behind it), 24px+ equivalent, never lean on audio (69% sound-off in public).
3. **Never put both the brand logo and heavy text on the same frame** (logo + captions cut ad memory 12%); show the logo in the end card only.
4. **Hashtags: 3-5 only**: breed (#goldenretrieverlover), niche (#dogmomgifts), product (#doghoodie), optional broad (#dogsoftiktok). Avoid 11+.
5. **Emoji: 0-3, one dog/paw emoji max in the caption**; do not rely on emoji for lift (evidence neutral to slightly negative on views).
6. **Capitalisation: Sentence case; capitalise at most 1-2 words for emphasis** ("Your dog NEEDS this" is borderline; "Meet the Corgi hoodie" is safer). Avoid full-caps lines over 4 words.
7. **Numerals and specificity in hooks**: "3 colours", "5 breeds", "10 sizes" (BuzzSumo 10, 5, 7, 15). Use numerals not spelled-out numbers.
8. **Second-person, warm, positive** ("your pup", "you'll want one for your corgi"); avoid accusatory/negative "you" (Stanford). Aim for high-arousal positive emotion: delight/awe/funny (amusement raised sharing in Berger & Milkman) and practical utility ("fits Frenchies up to 28 lb").
9. **CTA: a concrete save/share/comment ask**, not just "link in bio". Examples: "Tag the corgi person", "Which breed next?" (comment bait, 27% of top IG posts used a CTA; Buffer suggests "Save this for..."). For purchase: end card "Link in bio" plus breed name.
10. **Brazil / bilingual**: for the US, keep captions in English with one short Spanish phrase or a Spanish version of the CTA/end card where the audience is Hispanic (e.g. "Tu perro lo merece"); do not make full bilingual walls (50+ non-Hispanic viewers skew negative). For pt-BR, write natively informal (voce, pet, "cachorro", "pet shop" culture), a separate pt-BR caption and on-screen text, not machine-translated; use Brazilian spellings and 1-3 line cards; keep hashtags 3-5 mix of Portuguese (#cachorro, #dogmom, #petlovers) and breed terms. Test before scaling since evidence for pt-BR is weak.
11. **Pinterest variant**: title 35-45 visible chars with primary keyword first, description ~220 chars, <=5 keywords, hashtags optional.
