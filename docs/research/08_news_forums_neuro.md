## (b) Consolidated findings

### What is well evidenced (A/B sources)
1. **Reading speed.** Adult silent reading averages ~238 wpm (~4 words/s) for continuous text (Brysbaert 2019). Subtitle research uses 12-20 cps (~2.4-4 words/s); 20 cps is readable with no comprehension loss, 28 cps causes skipping and lower comprehension (items 1, 2, 30). For a 10 s reel with no audio dependence, budget conservatively because the viewer is also processing images: **~2.5-3 words/s as a ceiling, 2 words/s as target**.
2. **Slow text is not better.** At 12 cps viewers re-read ~half to two-thirds of subtitles and notice mismatches more (item 1): do not leave short text on screen far longer than needed. Short text, brief hold, then replace.
3. **Do not duplicate speech verbatim.** Identical text + narration reduces recall; reworded/abridged text helps (items 21, 22). Reels here are music-led with no speech, so text carries the message alone; if a voice or caption is added, the on-screen line should complement, not repeat.
4. **Arousal drives sharing.** Awe, anger, anxiety (high arousal) are shared more; sadness (low arousal) is shared less; practical utility and interest add independent lift; amusement raises share intent (items 11, 48). Positive + surprise dominates viral images (items 9, 10).
5. **Muted viewing is the default.** Multiple sources: 80-92% of mobile video views are muted (items 14, 15, 23); captions/overlay raise completion and recall, but prominent logo plus subtitles together lowered memory 12% (items 14, 15). Keep brand mark small.
6. **Mobile reading and attention.** People read a minority of on-page words (~20-28%) and mobile comprehension of hard text is half of desktop; short simple text is fine (items 3, 29). Mobile ad exposure is often <2 s and recall grows with every second (item 39).
7. **Surprise attracts gaze; curiosity aids memory** (item 8). TikTok's own guidance: show people early, core proposition in 3 s.
8. **Pet owners buy as self-expression/identity.** Pet attachment and anthropomorphism predict pet-clothing intent; owners spend on pets "as on themselves" (items 42, 45). Wording about "my dog = me/us" fits.
9. **Algorithm signals** (watch time, rewatch, shares) reward loops and share-worthy content (item 38).

### Weak/contested (C-D sources, use as test hypotheses)
- "First 3 s retention correlates 0.84", "+67% with specific first statement", captions "+15-40% watch time", "3 s hook" are vendor/self-reported without methodology.
- Pronoun ("you") use: one small preprint finds no consistent effect; natural insertion helps, forced hurts (item 46).
- Animated word-by-word captions: claimed better; academic view: moderate dynamics beat extreme (item 19).

### Wording patterns with some support
- Open loop/curiosity gap + visual pattern break (items 7, 8, 23).
- Relatable specific moment (emotional specificity) rather than generic claim (item 23).
- Identity call-out ("If you're a [X] dog parent") (item 23).
- Humor-disarming "POV" openers (items 27, 54).
- One CTA per video, specific, after the payoff (item 44).
- Caption preview ~150 chars, front-load keyword in first 60-80 characters (items 44, 53).
- Test two hook variants per video with everything else fixed; rotate hooks every 7-10 days (item 27).

## (c) Concrete wording implications for a dog apparel brand (10 s slideshow reels)

1. **Hook (frame 1-2 s), 3-5 words, large:** lead with the owner's identity or a relatable moment, not the product. Examples: "POV: your dog runs the house", "Your dog, but make it a hoodie", "Dog moms, this one's for you", "Wait for the last dog". Pairs curiosity + identity + humor.
2. **Mid cards (4-6 words, 2 s each, max 2 cards):** one idea each, benefit or joke, e.g., "Soft. Warm. Zero chew risk." / "Matching with your dog? Yes." Avoid specs lists. Do not repeat caption.
3. **Emotional register:** aim for high-arousal positive (awe, amusement, delight) and surprise; avoid sad/guilt framing (low-arousal, reduces sharing). Dog-as-self-extension angle: "Looks like him. Feels like you." Use "my/our dog" wording; owners treat dog spending as self-spending.
4. **Share trigger text:** a line that makes a viewer tag someone: "Tag the dog mom who needs this" / "Send this to your dog person." Shares and rewatch are signals the algorithm weights (item 38); utility/identity both predict sharing.
5. **CTA card (last 2-2.5 s, 3-6 words, y~1300-1450):** one action only: "Shop link in bio" or "Your dog's name on one?" Keep it specific and after the payoff; avoid stacking follow + comment + shop. Spend 5-8 words max if Snapchat style, but 3-5 is safer on a 10 s reel.
6. **Brand mark small:** logo prominent plus heavy text reduced ad memory by 12% in the Verizon/Publicis test; keep a corner or last-frame logo only.
7. **Caption (under 150 characters preview):** front-load a keyword (dog hoodie/dog mom shirt/breed name) plus an emotional line; 3-5 hashtags mixing broad and niche. Because TikTok parses on-screen text and captions for search, put the breed and product word in both.
8. **Do not narrate what is shown:** text on top of music-only reels carries the message. If a voice/caption repeats it, reword.
9. **Testing protocol:** ship two versions differing only in the hook text (3-5 words each), track 3-second hold, completion, shares and saves, and rotate hooks every ~7-10 days to prevent fatigue; treat vendor numbers (+67%, 0.84, 3x clicks) as hypotheses, not facts.
10. **Phone-check rule:** preview on a real phone at arm's length; if a card needs a second read, cut words or extend hold by 0.5 s.
